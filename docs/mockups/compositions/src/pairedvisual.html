Two values of x that give the same y, and the curve that explains why.

Case A

Question

Find y when x = −3.

Worked solution

  1. Substitute, keeping the brackets.
    y = (−3)2
  2. Evaluate.
    y = 9
Answer(−3, 9)

Case B

Question

Find y when x = 3.

Worked solution

    y = 32
  2. Evaluate.
    y = 9

Why the two agree

Squaring destroys the sign, so x and −x always give the same y. On the graph that is a mirror: the curve is symmetric about the y-axis.

y = (−3)2 = 32 = 9

The horizontal line y = 9 meets the curve at exactly the two points the two cases found. Any horizontal line above the vertex meets it twice, once on each side of the axis of symmetry — which is why a pair of answers is the expected result here, not a coincidence of these two numbers.

The two crossings are the same distance from the axis because the curve is built that way: every height is reached at x and at −x. Fold the page along the y-axis and the two cases land on each other.

The vertex is the one exception. At y = 0 the two crossings have met, and there is a single solution rather than a pair — which is the graph's way of saying that 0 is the only number that is its own negative.

Below the vertex the line misses the curve entirely. That is the same fact as “no real number squares to give a negative”, seen from the side.
