Why the two agree

Squaring destroys the sign, so x and −x always give the same y. On the graph that is a mirror: the curve is symmetric about the y-axis.

y = (−3)2 = 32 = 9

The horizontal line y = 9 meets the curve at exactly the two points the cases found. Any line above the vertex meets it twice, once on each side of the axis of symmetry.

At the vertex the two crossings have met, which is the single solution of x2 = 0. Below it the line misses the curve entirely — the same fact as “no real number squares to give a negative”.
