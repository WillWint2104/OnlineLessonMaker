A line of mathematics wider than its measure

Question

Expand and simplify (x + 2)(x − 3)(2x + 5)(x − 1), then find where the result is zero.

Worked solution

  1. Expand the first two brackets.
    x2 − x − 6
  2. Multiply by the third bracket and collect.
    2x3 + 3x2 − 17x − 30
  3. Now multiply by the fourth bracket, writing every product before collecting anything. This line does not wrap and it is not made smaller to fit — it scrolls inside its own region, and the tabs above are untouched by it.
    2x4 + 3x3 − 17x2 − 30x − 2x3 − 3x2 + 17x + 30 = 2x4 + (3 − 2)x3 − (17 + 3)x2 − (30 − 17)x + 30
  4. Collect like terms.
    2x4 + x3 − 20x2 − 13x + 30
  5. The zeros are the values that made each bracket zero.
    x = −5/2, −2, 1, 3
Answer2x4 + x3 − 20x2 − 13x + 30, zero at x = −5/2, −2, 1 and 3

Why it agrees

A simpler curve makes the same point: the zeros of an expression are exactly where its graph crosses the x-axis.

y = x2 − 4

Here the crossings are at x = −2 and x = 2, which are the values that make each bracket of (x + 2)(x − 2) zero.
