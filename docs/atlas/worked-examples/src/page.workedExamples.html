What the three show

Substitution never changes the rule — it only replaces x. The brackets keep the whole value together: a negative loses its sign when squared, a fraction is squared top and bottom, and a decimal below 1 gets smaller rather than larger.

What this shows

Undoing a square has to return both numbers that could have produced it. Losing the negative root here is the mistake that costs a solution in every quadratic afterwards.

Why they agree

Squaring destroys the sign, so x and −x always give the same y. The two cases are a mirror pair rather than two unrelated results.

The same fact, on the curve

The horizontal line y = 9 meets y = x2 at exactly the two points the cases found.

(−3, 9)   and   (3, 9)

They sit at equal distances either side of the y-axis. That mirror is what “squaring destroys the sign” looks like when it is drawn.

Any line above the vertex meets the curve twice, once on each side. At the vertex the two crossings have met, which is the single solution of x2 = 0. Below it the line misses the curve entirely — the same fact as “no real number squares to give a negative”.
