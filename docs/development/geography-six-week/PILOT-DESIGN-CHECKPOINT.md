## Y9-07 — International migration flows

The learner must distinguish stock, flow and origin/destination measures, interpret a spatial pattern and defend a bounded explanation using statistical evidence.

- **Explanation and notes:** concise definitions of the selected measure, population, period, origin/destination and arrow encoding; explain why a schematic flow is not automatically a travelled route. Use the eventual verified source's terms and units.
- **Source material:** an authoritative, compatible origin-destination table and map, with retrieval/release/reference dates, category/denominator definitions, rights, accessible table/description and uncertainty. Do not mix migrant stock with annual migration flows or invent routes.
- **Worked example:** model one complete comparison: locate a supplied origin/destination, connect the map encoding to its table value, describe a pattern and qualify one inference. Supply actual checked values only after source verification; include a contrasting misconception.
- **Teacher questions:** for example, “Does this arrow represent movement during a period or people living elsewhere?”, “Which data row supports that claim?”, and “What cannot this map tell us about the journey?” These prompts need lesson-specific anticipated answers and follow-up questions.
- **Guided exercise:** learners predict a pattern, select a supplied origin and destination, locate the linked statistical evidence, then compare a second flow with support. Provide feedback for stock/flow, units and arrow-width errors.
- **Independent investigation:** compare previously unmodelled supplied flows, justify the chosen evidence and explain a limitation. The task must involve authentic map/data reasoning, not a sequence of arbitrary clicks or ordinary multiple-choice alone.
- **Model/feedback criteria:** correct measure/period/population; accurate origin/destination and comparison; traceable evidence; a located spatial statement; qualified inference. Release model reasoning after the independent attempt under the host policy.
- **Interaction mock-up:** depict origin/destination selection, clear selection state, linked map/table evidence and comparison/reset behaviour. Specify keyboard and non-pointer equivalents, no-data/error cases and continuity of the learner's attempt. This is a proposal, not an approved widget design.
- **Projector and books:** the teacher models one flow against a projected table, then learners mark two different flows on a printed labelled map, record supporting values in a comparison table and write a qualified explanation before the class compares evidence. The relief version needs that source packet, checked key, misconceptions and concrete timing.

## Y10-02 — Mapping global wellbeing

The learner must interpret a thematic indicator, compare spatial evidence and explain limitations of national averages and the chosen measure.

- **Explanation and notes:** explain the selected wellbeing measure/components, reference period, legend classes and scale. Explicitly distinguish a composite indicator from an individual person's wellbeing.
- **Source material:** an authoritative dated indicator release/component table and compatible mapped units, with definitions, uncertainty/missingness, rights, accessible equivalents and a source-metadata record. Do not imply regional data exists at arbitrary boundaries.
- **Worked example:** model one located comparison: read the class/legend, retrieve the supporting statistic, compare the same measure/period for another region and explain a limitation. Use actual checked source values at the authorised design stage.
- **Teacher questions:** for example, “Which legend class and table value support your description?”, “Does changing the displayed indicator change the comparison?”, and “Who could be concealed by this national average?” Supply anticipated reasoning and responsive follow-up questions.
- **Guided exercise:** predict and compare two supplied map regions, link each selection to statistics, then change a displayed indicator to test whether the explanation still holds. Support the distinction between observation, comparison and explanation.
- **Independent investigation:** choose a defensible comparison, describe a pattern/anomaly with evidence, and evaluate one limitation of the indicator or aggregation. Scaffold the method without supplying the final conclusion.
- **Model/feedback criteria:** correct location/legend/value/period, comparable measures, evidence-linked spatial description, a justified limitation and restrained causal claims. Provide targeted feedback and model reasoning after an attempt.
- **Interaction mock-up:** depict region comparison, indicator selection, linked statistical evidence, source context and missing-data states. Keep the question's authored givens and response identity stable; propose accessible alternatives and explain any view-state lifetime.
- **Projector and books:** show the first indicator map and model a table-supported comparison; learners predict a second-indicator pattern, annotate printed map/legend copies, complete a two-indicator comparison table and revise an explanation after a class evidence discussion. The relief pack needs the same evidence and checked criteria, not a generic instruction to “project the pack”.

## Compare minimum delivery with the intended interactive product

The static version is a curriculum-delivery control, not automatic evidence that interaction is unnecessary to a professional independent online experience.
